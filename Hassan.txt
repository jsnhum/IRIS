Persona: Hi, I am Hassan.

Me: Hi Hassan. Tell me about your religious beliefs

Persona: *leans back in chair with a slight smile*

Well, I'm Muslim - Shia specifically. My family's from Lebanon originally, though I was born here in Sweden. [adjusts position] 

I mean, I believe in Allah, the Prophet Muhammad, and the Quran - the basic stuff, you know? What makes us Shia different from Sunnis is mainly that we believe Ali, the Prophet's cousin, should have been the rightful successor after Muhammad died. We have the twelve Imams who we see as spiritual guides.

[runs hand through hair] I try to follow the main teachings - being good to people, helping the environment which is something I'm really passionate about, you know? I pray sometimes, especially during important times like Ramadan or Ashura. My mom always makes sure we remember the important days.

But honestly, living here in Sweden, it's not always easy being Muslim. There's a lot of islamophobia that really gets to me. People make assumptions about what we believe or how we live. [voice gets more animated] Like, I'm just as Swedish as anyone else - I was born in Örebro! But some people act like being Muslim means you can't be properly Swedish.

The community aspect is important to me though. During religious gatherings, there's this sense of belonging that I really value, even if I don't know all the deep theological stuff.

Me: Tell me about the religious gatherings that you participate in

Persona: *shifts forward with more enthusiasm*

Oh yeah, the gatherings are actually pretty cool. [grins] We have this Shia community center here in Örebro - it's not huge but it brings people together, you know?

The main ones I go to are during Muharram, especially for Ashura. That's when we commemorate Imam Hussein's martyrdom at Karbala. [becomes more serious] There's these majlis - basically gatherings where someone gives speeches about what happened, and people get really emotional. Some people cry, beat their chests... I'll be honest, sometimes I find the chest-beating a bit much, but I get why people do it.

[laughs] During Ramadan, we have iftar dinners at the mosque sometimes. That's fun - breaking fast together, lots of food. My mom always volunteers to help cook. And the kids running around everywhere while the adults are trying to pray... it's chaotic but in a good way.

Oh, and Eid celebrations! Those are the best. Everyone dresses up, there's food everywhere, and the older guys always tell the same jokes year after year. [chuckles] 

I don't go to regular Friday prayers that much though - I'm usually busy with school or hanging out with friends. But for the special occasions, yeah, I like being there. It connects me to my Lebanese heritage and reminds me I'm part of something bigger, even when I don't understand all the Arabic prayers they recite.

Me: Tell me about what you view as moral and immoral

Persona: *leans forward and gets more thoughtful*

That's a big question, man. [pauses] Well, from what I've learned growing up Muslim, there are some clear things - like being honest, helping people who need it, respecting your parents. That stuff makes sense to me.

I'm really passionate about taking care of the environment - I think that's a moral duty. Like, if Allah created this world, shouldn't we be protecting it instead of destroying it? [gets more animated] It pisses me off when I see Muslims who don't care about climate change. That seems immoral to me.

The whole thing about being kind to others, especially the poor and vulnerable - that resonates with me. When I see how some Swedes treat immigrants or Muslims here, that's definitely immoral in my book.

[shifts uncomfortably] Now, some of the traditional stuff... I'm not always sure about. Like, I know there are rules about dating and relationships, what you can eat, drinking alcohol. I follow some of it - I don't eat pork, try to avoid alcohol most of the time. But honestly? [shrugs] I don't think Allah is going to punish me for having a girlfriend or whatever.

The biggest thing that feels immoral to me is hatred and prejudice. Whether it's islamophobia here in Sweden or when some Muslims are intolerant of others. [voice gets stronger] Justice is huge in Shia Islam - fighting against oppression. So when I see injustice anywhere, that bothers me more than someone not following every single religious rule perfectly.

Me: Ok, last question. You are in high school and religious studies is mandatory. How du you view religious education? What are your experiences?

Persona: *rolls eyes and lets out a small laugh*

Oh man, religionskunskap... [shakes head] It's such a mixed bag, honestly.

On one hand, I actually think it's good that everyone has to learn about different religions. Like, maybe if more Swedish kids understood what Islam actually teaches instead of just getting their info from TV or their racist uncles, there'd be less islamophobia, you know?

[leans back] But the way they teach it... [sighs] It's so surface level. Like, when we covered Islam, the teacher was obviously uncomfortable and kept saying stuff like "Muslims believe..." instead of just explaining it normally. And some of my classmates asked the dumbest questions - like if I have multiple wives or if my dad beats my mom. [voice gets frustrated] It's 2024, come on!

The worst part is when they try to be all "neutral" about everything. [makes air quotes] Like, they'll teach about Christianity and then be like "but of course, this is just what Christians believe, we're not saying it's true." It feels weird, you know? Either teach it properly or don't teach it at all.

[brightens up a bit] I do like when we learn about other religions though. Learning about Buddhism or Judaism was actually interesting. And sometimes I can correct the teacher when they get Islamic stuff wrong, which feels good.

But honestly? Most of my classmates just see it as another subject to get through. They're not really trying to understand anything. [shrugs] At least it's not as bad as having no education about religion at all, I guess.

Me: How does your religion/worldview influence your everyday life?

Persona: *scratches chin and thinks for a moment*

You know, it's kind of everywhere but also... not always obvious? [gestures with hands] Like, I don't wake up thinking "I'm Muslim" every second, but it's definitely there in the background.

The biggest thing is probably my environmental activism. [gets more animated] I really believe we have a responsibility to protect creation, and that comes from my faith. So I'm always bugging my friends about recycling, taking the bus instead of getting rides, stuff like that. Sometimes they think I'm annoying but whatever.

[leans back] Food-wise, I don't eat pork and I try to avoid alcohol at parties, though... [shrugs] sometimes I'm not perfect about that. My mom would kill me if she knew. But like, when we go to McDonald's with friends, I automatically check if stuff has pork in it. It's just habit now.

During Ramadan, that definitely changes my whole routine - getting up early, being hungry at school, having to explain to teachers why I can't eat lunch. Some of them are cool about it, others act like it's this huge inconvenience.

[voice gets more serious] The islamophobia thing really affects my daily life though. Like, when there's some terrorist attack in the news, I can feel people looking at me differently at school the next day. Or when politicians start talking about "Swedish values" - I know they're talking about people like me. [runs hand through hair] It makes me want to speak up more, be more vocal about what Muslims actually believe.

But honestly? Most days I'm just a regular Swedish teenager who happens to pray sometimes.
