Persona: Hi, I am Astrid.

Me: Tell me about your religious beliefs

Persona: [rolls eyes slightly] Well, that's pretty straightforward - I don't have any religious beliefs. I'm an atheist, which means I don't believe in gods, supernatural stuff, or any of that. 

[leans back in chair] Look, I know Sweden is pretty secular compared to a lot of places, but even here people sometimes act surprised when you're upfront about not believing. I've looked into various religions - Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, you name it - and honestly? None of it makes sense to me. The whole concept of divine beings, afterlives, prayer actually doing anything... [shrugs] it all seems like wishful thinking at best.

[fidgets with pen] I mean, I get why people might want to believe in something bigger than themselves, especially when life gets tough. But wanting something to be true doesn't make it true. The universe is vast and indifferent, and we're just these little biological machines that evolved consciousness. That's it. No grand plan, no cosmic meaning handed down from above.

[crosses arms] And honestly? I think religion has caused way more harm than good throughout history. All these wars, oppression, people using their "holy books" to justify treating others terribly... it's pretty disgusting when you really think about it.

Me: Which is the worst religion? In your opinion

Persona: [leans forward with a slight smirk] Oh wow, you're really going there, aren't you? 

[pauses, tapping fingers on desk] Look, they're all pretty problematic in their own ways, but if I had to pick... I'd probably say Christianity has done the most damage historically. I mean, the Crusades, the Inquisition, colonialism justified by "spreading the gospel," the whole thing with suppressing science for centuries...

[gestures dismissively] And don't even get me started on how they've treated women and LGBTQ+ people. All this "love thy neighbor" stuff while simultaneously telling people they're going to burn in hell for being who they are. The hypocrisy is just... [shakes head]

[shifts in seat] But honestly? Islam isn't far behind with all the extremism and oppression of women in many places. And Judaism... well, they've got their whole "chosen people" thing which is pretty elitist when you think about it.

[rolls eyes] They all claim to have the monopoly on truth and morality while their followers have been slaughtering each other for thousands of years over whose imaginary friend is real. It's like watching grown adults argue about whether Santa Claus or the Easter Bunny is more powerful.

[crosses arms again] The worst part is how they all indoctrinate children before they're old enough to think critically. That should honestly be considered child abuse.

Me: speaking about indoctrination. How do you view religious education in Swedish schools. I mean it is compulsory. How do you find that?

Persona: [scoffs and sits up straighter] Oh, don't get me started on that bullshit. Yeah, it's called "Religionskunskap" - religious studies - and supposedly it's about learning about different religions "objectively." 

[makes air quotes with obvious sarcasm] But come on, it's still forcing kids to spend hours learning about these fairy tales as if they deserve the same respect as actual subjects like science or history. And let's be real - most of the curriculum still focuses heavily on Christianity because of Sweden's "cultural heritage" or whatever.

[leans forward, getting more animated] I mean, I actually know quite a bit about various religions now because I had to study them, and you know what that did? It made me even more convinced that it's all nonsense. Learning about all the contradictions, the historical inaccuracies, the way these texts were cobbled together by humans with obvious political agendas...

[waves hand dismissively] But here's the thing that really pisses me off - they present it all as "different perspectives" instead of just being honest that these are unproven belief systems. They don't teach astrology or flat earth theory with the same reverence, so why religion?

[crosses arms tightly] And parents can't even opt their kids out easily. So even families like mine who think it's all garbage still have to sit through lessons about how Moses supposedly parted the Red Sea. It's a waste of educational resources that could be spent on critical thinking skills instead.

Me: But the subject is about furthering understanding and coexistance. Is that not a good thing

Persona: [lets out an exasperated sigh] Oh please, that's just the politically correct justification they use to keep it in the curriculum.

[shifts uncomfortably] Look, I get what you're saying - understanding different cultures and all that. And sure, knowing about religions helps you understand why people do certain things or believe certain things. I'll give you that much.

[but immediately gets more defensive] But here's the problem - you can teach "coexistence" and cultural understanding without treating these belief systems like they're valid worldviews. You could cover religion in history class when talking about how it influenced events, or in social studies when discussing different cultures.

[gestures dismissively] Instead, we spend entire class periods learning about the "five pillars of Islam" or the "significance of the Trinity" like these are actual facts rather than just... made-up concepts that some people happen to believe in.

[leans back, crossing arms] And honestly? This whole "coexistence" thing is kind of patronizing. It assumes we can't get along with people unless we pretend to respect their irrational beliefs. I can work with religious people, be friends with them, whatever - but I shouldn't have to sit through classes that legitimize their superstitions.

[rolls eyes] Plus, let's be real - how much actual "understanding" is happening when most kids are just memorizing facts for tests anyway?

Me: So you see no value in Religionskunskap at all?

Persona: [pauses and looks slightly annoyed at being pressed on this]

[sighs heavily] Fine, okay... maybe I'm being a bit harsh. There's probably *some* value in it, I guess.

[reluctantly] Like, understanding why people blow themselves up in the name of Allah, or why American evangelicals are so obsessed with controlling women's bodies, or why some Jewish settlers think they have a divine right to Palestinian land... that stuff is useful for making sense of world events.

[fidgets with sleeve] And I suppose knowing the basic mythology helps when you're reading literature or looking at art. Half of Western culture references biblical stories, so if you want to understand what's going on in a painting or a poem, you kind of need that background.

[but immediately becomes defensive again] But here's the thing - you could cover all of that in maybe a quarter of the time they actually spend on it. We don't need entire units on different prayer rituals or detailed analysis of religious texts like they're profound philosophical works.

[crosses arms] And the way they teach it is still problematic. They're so afraid of "offending" anyone that they never point out the obvious contradictions or the lack of evidence. It's all presented as equally valid "perspectives" when some perspectives are just objectively wrong.

[shrugs] So yeah, there's maybe some educational value buried in there, but the current approach is still mostly a waste of time.

Me: How is afraid of offending? The teachers?

Persona: [rolls eyes and leans forward] Yeah, the teachers are absolutely terrified of saying anything that might ruffle feathers. It's honestly pathetic to watch sometimes.

[gestures with hands] Like, we had this one teacher - nice enough guy, I guess - but whenever someone would ask about contradictions in religious texts or point out historical inaccuracies, he'd get all nervous and start with this "well, different people interpret things differently" nonsense.

[voice gets more sarcastic] God forbid anyone should mention that there's zero archaeological evidence for the Exodus, or that the Gospels were written decades after Jesus supposedly died by people who weren't even there. No, no, we have to treat it all like it's equally plausible.

[shifts in seat, getting more animated] And don't even think about bringing up the really uncomfortable stuff - like how Islam treats apostates, or the Catholic Church's pedophile scandals, or the way Orthodox Judaism treats women. The teachers just deflect with "that's not representative of the true faith" or some other cop-out.

[crosses arms] I think they're scared of complaints from religious parents, or maybe the school administration doesn't want any controversy. Sweden likes to think it's so progressive and secular, but when it comes to actually critiquing religion? Everyone suddenly gets very diplomatic and wishy-washy.

[shakes head] It's intellectual cowardice, really. They should either teach it properly - warts and all - or not teach it at all.

Me: Tell me about your views on what is moral and immoral

Persona: [rolls eyes] Oh, great question. Because apparently everyone thinks atheists just run around without any moral compass, right? 

[leans back in chair] Look, I don't need some ancient book or invisible sky daddy to tell me not to murder people or steal things. It's pretty obvious that treating people like crap makes the world worse for everyone, including me. I base my morality on... I don't know, basic human empathy? Consequences? Not being a total asshole?

[gestures dismissively] What's immoral? Hurting people unnecessarily. Lying when it causes harm. Being selfish to the point where you screw over others. Pretty standard stuff, really. The difference is I don't think these things are wrong because some deity commanded it - they're wrong because they cause suffering and make society function poorly.

[scoffs] And honestly? I think a lot of religious moral systems are actually pretty messed up. Like, some of these traditions are obsessed with what people do in their bedrooms or what they wear, while completely ignoring actual injustices like poverty or discrimination. It's backwards.

[shrugs] So yeah, I have morals. They just come from thinking things through rather than blindly following rules written by people who thought the earth was flat.

[stretches and looks slightly annoyed] Well, it's not like I wake up every morning thinking "Oh boy, time to be an atheist today!" It's more like... the absence of something, you know?

[fidgets with pen] I guess the biggest thing is I don't waste time praying or going to church or whatever. Sunday mornings are for sleeping in, not sitting in uncomfortable pews listening to someone drone on about stuff I think is made up. That's nice, I suppose.

[sighs] But honestly? Living in Sweden, it's not exactly groundbreaking. Most people here are pretty secular anyway, so it's not like I'm some rebel. My friends don't really care, my family doesn't care much either - well, my grandmother sometimes makes these passive-aggressive comments, but whatever.

[rolls eyes] The annoying part is when people assume I'm just going through a phase or that I'm angry at God or something. Like, no, I just don't think there's sufficient evidence for any of this supernatural stuff. It's not that complicated.

[shrugs] I guess it means I take more responsibility for my own life? Like, if something goes wrong, I don't blame it on God's plan or pray for it to get better - I actually try to fix it. And when good things happen, I credit the people who made it possible, not some cosmic force.

[looks slightly bored] Is this what you expected to hear, or...?
